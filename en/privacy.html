Second Chance is a free, ad-free iOS app that shows “zu verschenken” (free giveaway) boxes left on the street in Aachen on a map. This policy explains which personal data is processed, on what legal basis, and for how long. It is deliberately short and honest: nothing is promised that the app does not actually do.

2. Overview: what the app deliberately does not do

  • No advertising, no tracking, no analytics or crash-reporting SDKs (no Firebase, Sentry, Crashlytics, Mixpanel, no IDFA, no App Tracking Transparency dialog).
  • No name and no email from “Sign in with Apple” (no scopes are requested, so Apple shares neither name nor email).
  • No location history is stored.
  • No prices, no item names, no chat between users.
  • The identity of anyone who posted, confirmed or reported a box is never shown to other users.

3. Accounts: anonymous, and optionally with Apple

On first launch the app silently creates an anonymous account (a random identifier/UUID at our processor Supabase). Without signing in and without a name, you can browse the map, confirm boxes (“still has items” / “empty”), record a take, create saved searches and receive notifications.

Only to post a box yourself is “Sign in with Apple” required. This links an Apple identity to your existing anonymous account in place (same id, points and history). Apple shares only an anonymous Apple user identifier with us — no name and no email.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (providing the functions you use / performing the user relationship).

4. Processing in detail

For each processing we state the purpose, the data involved and the legal basis.

4.1 Showing the map

Purpose: display the map and boxes. Map tiles are loaded from the provider OpenFreeMap (section 6); this necessarily sends your IP address to that provider and requests the map area shown.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (providing the map) and Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (legitimate interest in a working map).

OpenFreeMap is operated by Hyperknot Software Kft. (Hungary, EU); delivery is via dedicated servers, partly through Cloudflare as a content delivery network (CDN). According to its own information, OpenFreeMap stores no IP addresses in its access logs; IP addresses may be kept in error logs for up to 7 days and, during a security incident, for up to 30 days. OpenFreeMap is based in the EU, so there is no third-country transfer here.

4.1a Place search and addresses

Purpose: finding places and addresses. When you type in the map search field, your input is sent to Apple (MapKit, MKLocalSearch) for a place search. When posting, the box coordinate is passed to Apple to determine the street name (reverse geocoding); when you tap “Route”, the coordinate is passed to the Maps app.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

4.2 Location

The app uses your location only while in use (“When In Use”, iOS permission), for three things: centring the map on you, determining the box location when posting, and checking you are actually on site for a confirmation or take (≤ 100 m, accuracy ≤ 65 m).

  • What is stored: when posting, the box coordinate (which is public — that is the point of the app). For map centring and the on-site check, your location is not stored on the server; the coordinate of a confirmation/take is passed to the server only for the check and is not kept.
  • What does not happen: no location history is stored and your location is shared with no one. The “Always” location permission is not requested in this version.
  • On the device, a confirmation/take not yet uploaded (e.g. offline) may briefly sit in a local queue (incl. the on-site coordinate); that entry is deleted as soon as the action has been processed. It leaves the device only as the check request described above.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (map centring, posting, on-site check as core functions) and Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (legitimate interest in preventing abuse/manipulation of the on-site check).

4.3 Box photos — including photos with third parties

When posting you take a photo of the box with the in-app camera. Such a photo may unintentionally show a passer-by, a vehicle number plate or a doorbell/name plate — all of which are personal data.

  • Masking on the device before upload: right after the shutter, the app detects faces, people and plate-like text lines on the device (Apple Vision) and covers those regions opaquely (no blur, no fine pixelation that could be reversed). Beforehand, you can cover further regions yourself.
  • Only the masked photo is uploaded. The unmasked original stays in the device’s memory only; it is never written to disk, a cache, the photo library, logs or crash reports, and is discarded when the flow ends or is cancelled.
  • Metadata: all EXIF data (especially GPS) is removed before upload; the photo is resized/compressed on the device.
  • Where the photo lives: the masked photo is stored in a public store (“box-photos” at Supabase, EU region Frankfurt) and is visible to everyone via the map. This is intended — a box photo is meant to be public.

Legal basis: for publishing the (masked) box photo and processing any third-party data remaining despite masking: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (legitimate interest in operating the giveaway-map service, balanced against the rights of third parties, with on-device masking, EXIF removal and fast removal on report as the safeguards). For providing your own contribution: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

Note on automatic masking: detection and covering run automatically on your device. It is a safeguard, not a decision producing legal effects concerning you (no automated individual decision under Art. 22 GDPR, no profiling). Detection cannot catch every case; it is deliberately tuned to over- rather than under-mask.

4.4 Contributions: confirmations and takes

Purpose: keep the map current (how fresh a box is, whether it is empty). We store the fact of a confirmation (“still has items”/“empty”) or a take, linked to your account id and the time — not the tap coordinate (see 4.2). These entries are not visible to other users; they feed the display only in aggregate.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

4.5 Saved searches

Purpose: you create a saved search (categories + a centre + a radius) to be told when a matching box appears. The centre is a coordinate you pick and may be sensitive (e.g. near your home). It is stored only in your own account and read server-side solely to match new boxes.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

4.6 Push notifications

If you allow notifications, the app registers a device token with Apple Push Notification service (APNs) and stores it at Supabase. When a box matching one of your saved searches appears, a server function sends a notification via APNs. Its text contains only a category and a rounded distance (e.g. “Bücher in 300 m”) — never the poster’s identity, no exact coordinates and no item names.

You only receive push notifications if you (1) allow notifications for the app in iOS and (2) create a saved search yourself; you can withdraw or delete either at any time.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (delivering the saved-search matches you set up). Delivery also requires your iOS system permission for notifications, which you can revoke at any time in iOS Settings, ending delivery. A token reported invalid by APNs is deleted.

4.7 Reports and moderation

You can report a box or photo (reason + optional note). A privacy report (reason: people, plates or names visible) hides the photo immediately and moves it to a private quarantine store. We review every report (target: within 48 hours) and decide: restore the photo, delete the photo (box stays), remove the box, or block the poster. The decision is recorded on the report.

For your in-app report we store the reason, the note, your account id, the time and the decision. To the poster your report is anonymous.

People without the app can report content via the contact form on the website (form). In that case we store the contact email they provide (so we can reply), the reason, a description, — if given — the street/location of the box, and optionally a name (to state the notifier under Art. 16(2) of the Digital Services Act; giving a name is voluntary). For abuse prevention we also briefly process a per-email count and a salted hash (SHA-256) of the IP address (the IP address itself is not stored). The form sets no cookies and embeds no third parties; the submission goes directly to our server function at Supabase (EU, Frankfurt). These form reports (including a name if given) are kept for the review/audit trail; the abuse count/IP hash is deleted after at most 2 days.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(c) GDPR (notice-and-action under Art. 16 of the Digital Services Act) and Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (legitimate interest in removing infringing content and protecting third parties; for the contact email, also to answer the report).

4.8 Hiding a poster

You can hide boxes from a particular poster. Technically a pair of your account id and the other person’s (server-resolved) account id is stored; you never learn who the person is.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) and (f) GDPR.

4.9 Points, settings, language

Your account also holds: contribution points (a game element), your notification settings, the accepted posting-rules version and your language/locale (so notifications arrive in the right language). These are tied to your account only.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

4.10 Feedback

Purpose: via “Feedback” (Profile) you can send us a short message with an idea, a problem or praise; an email address for a reply is optional. We store the topic, your message, the optional contact email, a technical context (app version, iOS version, device model and language) and your account id. The device model is the hardware identifier (e.g. “iPhone16,1”), not your device name. The account id serves a daily limit and ensures your feedback is deleted together with your account. Your feedback is visible only to the operator; it is shown to no one and not forwarded by email.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (legitimate interest in improving the app and answering your message); the optional email address you provide voluntarily.
Retention: 12 months; deleted together with your account.

5. Source and disclosure: third-party photos

We partly process data not provided by you: namely people or vehicles that might unintentionally appear on a box photo (Art. 14 GDPR). Such data — if it escapes the automatic and manual masking — is shown on the public map. We counter this with on-device masking before upload, EXIF removal and fast removal on report (section 4.7). Affected third parties may contact us at any time (section 1) or report the content (website home page); we remove affected photos.

Individually notifying such incidentally depicted people is impossible for us, or possible only with disproportionate effort (we do not know their identity or contact details); we therefore rely on Art. 14(5)(b) GDPR and instead make this information publicly available and remove affected photos promptly on report.

9. Deleting your account

You can delete your account at any time in the app (Profile → Settings), including a purely anonymous account. Your personal records (confirmations, takes, saved searches, push token, reports, points, hidden posters, any Apple link) are deleted and any Apple grant is revoked at Apple. Boxes you posted stay on the map, anonymised (the link to your account is removed) so the map is not torn up; their photos are not deleted at that moment but go by the normal expiry path (box leaves the map → after ~30 days). So an anonymised box’s photo is never kept longer than any other box’s.

10. Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to: access (Art. 15), rectification (Art. 16), erasure (Art. 17), restriction (Art. 18), data portability (Art. 20), and to object to processing based on Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR on grounds relating to your particular situation (Art. 21).

To exercise a right, a message to sec0nd.chance.app.mine@gmail.com is enough. As the accounts are pseudonymous (no names/email), we may have to ask you for details that let us identify your account; otherwise we may be unable to fulfil a request (Art. 11 GDPR).

11. No automated individual decision-making

There is no automated individual decision-making within the meaning of Art. 22 GDPR and no profiling. The only automated processing is the on-device masking of faces, people and plate-like text on photos (section 4.3); it protects third parties and produces no legal effect concerning you.

12. No tracking; access to your device (§ 25 TDDDG)

The app contains no tracking, advertising or analytics technology and no App Tracking Transparency dialog. This website sets no cookies, loads no external fonts, scripts or third-party content and embeds no trackers.

Access to your device under § 25 TDDDG (German Telecommunications-Digital-Services Data Protection Act): the app only stores or reads information on your device that is strictly necessary for the functions you have explicitly requested (app settings, a short-lived offline queue, a photo cache and — only if you enable notifications — your device’s push token). This is permitted without separate consent under § 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG; the push token is moreover used only after you grant notifications in iOS. No cookies or comparable techniques are used for analytics or advertising purposes.
